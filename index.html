Universe · Deep dive

What Actually Happens Around a Black Hole?

Black holes are regions where gravity becomes so strong that an event horizon forms—a boundary beyond which light cannot escape to the outside universe.

In this articleGravity changes spacetimeThe event horizonAccretion disks
Key takeaway: A black hole is not a cosmic vacuum cleaner; the defining feature is an event horizon and the extreme curvature of spacetime around it.

Gravity changes spacetime

In general relativity, gravity is described through the curvature of spacetime. Near a black hole these effects become extreme.

The event horizon

The event horizon is a boundary in spacetime. Once something crosses it, no signal traveling at or below light speed can return to a distant observer.

Accretion disks

Some black holes are surrounded by hot gas and dust forming an accretion disk. This material can become extremely hot and bright even though light from inside the event horizon cannot escape.

Cosmology · Deep dive

The Universe Is Mostly Made of Something We Cannot See

Dark matter is inferred from gravitational effects even though it does not emit, absorb or reflect enough light for direct observation.

In this articleThe missing-gravity problemHow much?What is it?
Key takeaway: Dark matter is an inference from gravitational evidence; its identity remains an open scientific question.

The missing-gravity problem

Stars and galaxies move in ways that cannot be fully explained by visible matter alone. Gravitational lensing gives another clue because mass can bend light.

How much?

NASA's current overview estimates ordinary matter at about 5%, dark matter at about 27%, and dark energy at about 68% of the universe's energy budget.

What is it?

Scientists have proposed candidates, but no candidate has been confirmed as the identity of dark matter. That uncertainty is part of the scientific story.

Exoplanets · Deep dive

Thousands of Worlds Orbit Other Stars

An exoplanet is a planet outside our Solar System. NASA's catalog has passed the 6,000-confirmed-planet milestone.

In this articleHow are they found?Could any host life?
Key takeaway: Finding an exoplanet is only the first step; whether a world can support life requires much more evidence.

How are they found?

Many exoplanets are detected indirectly. The transit method looks for repeated dips in a star's brightness when a planet crosses in front of it.

Could any host life?

Earth is the only world currently known to host life. A planet being in a habitable zone does not prove that life exists there.

Source: NASA Exoplanet Exploration
