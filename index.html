Universe · Deep dive

What Actually Happens Around a Black Hole?

Black holes are regions where gravity becomes so strong that an event horizon forms—a boundary beyond which light cannot escape to the outside universe.

Gravity changes spacetime

In Einstein's general relativity, gravity is described through the curvature of spacetime. Massive objects change the geometry around them. Near a black hole, these effects become extreme.

Time does not tick at the same rate everywhere

NASA explains that gravitational time dilation is measurable even on Earth: clocks at different gravitational potentials tick at slightly different rates. Near a black hole, the effect becomes much stronger.

The event horizon

The event horizon is not a physical surface. It is a boundary in spacetime. Once something crosses it, no signal traveling at or below light speed can return to a distant observer.

Accretion disks

Some black holes are surrounded by hot gas and dust forming an accretion disk. This material can become extremely hot and bright even though light from inside the event horizon cannot escape.

Cosmology · Deep dive

The Universe Is Mostly Made of Something We Cannot See

Dark matter is inferred from gravitational effects even though it does not appear to emit, absorb or reflect enough light for direct observation.

The missing-gravity problem

Stars in galaxies and galaxies in clusters move in ways that cannot be fully explained by visible matter alone. Gravitational lensing provides another clue because mass can bend light.

How much?

NASA's current overview estimates ordinary matter at about 5%, dark matter at about 27%, and dark energy at about 68% of the universe's total energy budget. The identity of dark matter remains unknown.

Candidates are still candidates

Scientists have proposed several possible particles and other explanations, but there is no confirmed identity for dark matter. That uncertainty is part of the scientific story.

Thousands of Worlds Orbit Other Stars

An exoplanet is a planet outside our Solar System. NASA's continuously updated catalog has passed the 6,000-confirmed-planet milestone.

How are they found?

Many exoplanets are detected indirectly. The transit method looks for repeated dips in a star's brightness when a planet crosses in front of it. Other methods measure the motion of a star caused by an orbiting planet's gravity.

Could any host life?

Earth is the only world currently known to host life. Scientists search for planets with potentially interesting conditions, including worlds in habitable zones, but a habitable-zone location does not prove that life exists there.

Why keep searching?

Each planetary system gives scientists another data point for understanding how planets form and how common different planetary environments may be.
